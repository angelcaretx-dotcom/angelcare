Every ride follows the same seven steps: request, price, confirmation, schedule, assign, drive, bill. Each step below says who does it and where to click.

1. A request arrives

A customer books on the website, or a facility books in its portal. The office gets an email "New trip request … log in to view" and the request shows on the dashboard under "New trip requests to review". The email never carries rider details or a price. It has two links: Check and send the price opens the price page of step 2 (for Administrators), and Review in Admin opens the request itself. Every request is also in Trip requests.

Read it, then link it to a passenger (choose an existing one or add a new one). A trip can't be scheduled until the request has a passenger. Set the status to Reviewed.

If the rider is already a passenger, link them before a price is sent. The passenger's payer is how the system knows a ride is paid by Medicaid or a broker, and refuses to email a price for it.

2. Check and send the price

Done by an Administrator. Press Check and send the price in the office email, or open the request and press Email the price: both open the same page. The price is already worked out there, from an estimate of the miles. Nothing is sent until you press the Email button.

Check the miles. The estimate is made inside the system from the two ZIP codes. It is approximate, not the driving distance, and it can be several miles off. Press Open the route in Google Maps (it opens in a new tab in your own browser), read the driving miles one way, type them in the miles box and press Work out the price. When a price was already sent for the request, the box starts with that price's miles instead. When there is no estimate (an address without a ZIP code, a ZIP code outside the Texas table, or both addresses in the same ZIP code) the page says why, and you take the miles from the map.

Wait-and-return rides. The first price shown has no waiting time in it. Type the wait the customer expects, in minutes, and press Work out the price.

Check the price. Read each line. "How this price is worked out" lists the rules of the rate card that apply to this request.

Confirm and send. Tick both boxes: "I checked the miles on the map." and "The customer pays for this ride. It is not paid by Medicaid, a broker or a facility account." Then press the Email button. Without both ticks nothing is sent and nothing is saved. Both ticks are kept in the audit log with the price. The customer receives the total with each part listed (the fees, the miles and any waiting time) and their reference number, and the request becomes Contacted. The email never repeats their name, an address, the date or the time, and it does not name the service.

Facility bookings. For a request booked in the facility portal the page says "Booked through the facility portal by" and the facility's name. Decide each time who pays. Email a price only when the person who booked pays for the ride. If the facility is invoiced, do not email a price.

Riders whose trips are paid by Medicaid or a broker are never sent a price: they owe nothing. The page refuses when the linked passenger has such a payer. For a request with no passenger linked yet, the system cannot know, so check before you tick the second box.

The price comes from the private-pay rate card in effect on the day of the ride: the booking fee, the loading fee, the miles and any wait time for that service. Nobody types a price by hand. Rates are never shown on the website. {% if can_quote %} Rates are kept in Rate cards; to change a price, add a new card with a later start date, so prices already sent stay as they were. Every price sent is listed in Prices emailed. {% endif %}

3. The customer confirms

The email tells the customer the ride is not booked yet. They confirm by calling or by sending a website message with their reference number. Nothing is scheduled until they do.

4. Schedule the trip

Go to Add trip and choose the request under "Trip request". The passenger, service, addresses and pickup time fill in. Save.

The request becomes Scheduled and the customer sees "Confirmed" in My trips. For a round trip the ride back is created at the same time: at the time they asked for, or at an estimated time if they will call or the driver waits. The miles from the emailed price are copied to the trip, so the invoice charges what the customer was told.

5. Assign a driver and a vehicle

Open the Day board for the day of the ride. Trips without a driver are at the top under "Needs a driver". Choose a driver and a vehicle and press Assign. You can also choose them on the trip's own page, under Assignment.

The system refuses an assignment that isn't safe to make, and says why: a driver who isn't Active, whose license has expired or who is missing a required credential; a vehicle that is out of service, or whose registration, inspection or insurance has expired.

On a wait-and-return ride the driver who takes the rider there also brings them back.

6. The driver completes the ride

Drivers use the driver app on their own phone, at {% url 'driver_app:home' %} on this site. They sign in with their email or phone number and a password. An Administrator creates the login on the driver's page ("Create app login") and hands over the printed sheet.

Start of day: the driver checks the vehicle against the checklist and types the odometer. No trip can start before that. A failed item takes the vehicle out of service and emails the office.

Each trip: the app shows today's trips in order with the rider, the addresses, directions and a call button. The driver taps one step at a time: En route, Arrived at pickup, Rider picked up (the rider signs on the phone, or the driver gives the reason they couldn't), Rider dropped off. If the rider isn't there, the driver records a No-show or a cancellation at the door, with a reason.

Every tap saves the time and the phone's location. The trip turns In progress on the first step and Completed on drop-off by itself: nobody in the office changes the status. The Day board shows each trip's latest step when you refresh it.

End of day: the driver types the odometer again.

7. Proof and payment

The trip's page keeps the timeline, the signature and a Proof sheet (PDF) you can download at any time.

{% if can_bill %} In Create invoices, choose Private pay and the dates. Each completed ride gets a Draft invoice priced from the same rate card as the emailed price. Check it, mark it Sent, and mark it Paid when the money arrives. {% else %} An Administrator creates the invoices: each completed ride is priced from the same rate card as the emailed price. {% endif %} A wait-and-return ride is invoiced on the wait the driver app recorded, so it can differ from the wait the customer expected.
